### Investigation: why iter1 fails the grader but looks perfect locally

Reported symptom (real grader): iter1-runtime TIMES OUT, scores ~10/100, passes 2/7.
Local harness (./evaluate.sh on data/ppsurf): 10/10 VALID, mean compression 75.12%.
=> The local evaluation is NOT representative; it hides the real failure.

ROOT CAUSE - runtime/scale gap, not the simplification quality:
1. Dataset too small. data/ppsurf maxes at 9,636 vertices. The grader allows up
   to ~1.1M vertices / 2.1M faces. Measured solver runtime (CPython) is ~linear
   at ~0.13 ms/vertex:
     1428 v -> 0.15s,  3803 v -> 0.38s,  9636 v -> 1.29s.
   Empirically, on a synthesized 154,146-vertex manifold the SAME solver takes
   44.5 s and 756 MB - already 2x over the 21 s budget at 1/7 of the max size.
   Extrapolated: ~617k v -> ~3 min, ~1.1M v -> minutes. pypy3 (the grader) is
   faster than CPython but cannot close a >10x gap; the largest meshes still
   blow the 21 s limit.
2. Harness timeout too lenient: evaluate_dataset.py uses 300 s vs the grader's
   21 s, so timeouts never surface locally.
3. No runtime visibility: the harness never reports per-mesh solve time, so a
   slow-but-correct solver looks perfect.
4. Locale bug in evaluate.sh: under a comma-decimal locale, printf '%.4f' on the
   compression rate fails ("invalid number") and the log is mislabeled
   compr-75,0000; the awk improvement comparison can also misbehave.

WHY THIS MAPS TO "10/100, 2/7": on the grader a timeout yields no/garbage output
=> invalid => 0 compression for that mesh. Only the smallest meshes finish in
21 s -> a handful pass, and the mean compression collapses toward ~10.

CONCLUSION: the binding problem is RUN-TIME COMPLEXITY at scale plus the lack of
a self-imposed time budget in the solver - NOT the per-mesh compression logic
(which is fine: ~75% on small meshes). Fix the scaffolding to expose runtime,
then make the solver degrade gracefully within the budget.

  A3. Add a configurable grader-style time budget (default 21 s) and FLAG/mark
      scenarios that exceed it, so a slow solver can no longer look perfect.
  A4. Add large synthetic stress meshes (datasets/prepare_stress.py, midpoint

      them out of the compression leaderboard (separate dir, gitignored).

  B1. Internal wall-clock budget in simplify(): stop collapsing before the limit
      and always emit a VALID mesh. This alone converts grader timeouts (0) into
      valid partial compression - the single biggest expected win.
  B2. Cut the per-collapse constant (array-based adjacency, fewer set ops/allocs,
      lazy deletion) so more collapses fit in the budget -> higher compression
      at scale.
  B3. Keep the existing validity gates (link condition, normal-flip cos, cluster
      Hausdorff proxy) so SSIM/Hausdorff stay satisfied; preserve ~75% on the
      small set (no regression there).

(more compression within budget) > A1 (correct bookkeeping).
